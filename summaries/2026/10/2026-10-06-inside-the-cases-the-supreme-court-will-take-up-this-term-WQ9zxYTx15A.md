# Inside the Cases the Supreme Court Will Take Up This Term

- **Published:** 2026-10-06
- **Channel:** CBS News
- **Video:** [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WQ9zxYTx15A)

## Summary
The Supreme Court's new term begins, featuring major cases on climate change responsibility, voter ID laws, gun control, and immigration, alongside ongoing tension among the justices regarding shadow docket decisions.

## Major Developments
### 1. Climate Change Liability Cases
The Supreme Court is reviewing whether federal law prevents state and local governments from holding oil companies like ExxonMobil and Suncor Energy responsible for climate change damages, given that carbon dioxide release is regulated under the federal Clean Air Act.

**Why it matters:** This case will determine whether fossil fuel companies can be sued in state courts for climate change impacts or if federal regulations preempt such liability.

### 2. Voter ID Laws and Citizenship Proof
The case Republican National Committee v. Mi Familia Vota examines an Arizona voter ID law requiring citizenship proof to register to vote, and whether states can remove people without citizenship evidence from voter rolls up to 90 days before federal elections.

**Why it matters:** It tests the constitutionality of strict voter registration requirements and state authority to clean voter rolls close to federal elections.

### 3. Second Amendment and Semicomplete Rifle Bans
Viramontes v. Cook County challenges the constitutionality of banning individuals from owning semi-automatic rifles, specifically the AR-15.

**Why it matters:** A decision could impact laws in 11 states that currently prohibit assault weapons.

### 4. Immigration Policy Cases
The court is reviewing three immigration cases addressing whether terminated asylum status invalidates citizenship claims, the lawfulness of third-country deportations, and the legality of mandatory immigrant detention policies.

**Why it matters:** These rulings directly impact federal immigration enforcement policies and the rights of asylum seekers.

## Key Takeaways
- The Supreme Court's new term covers high-profile issues including climate change, voting rights, gun control, and immigration.
- Liberal Supreme Court justices have publicly voiced frustration over the court frequently issuing emergency opinions via the shadow docket without explanation.
- The court issued 31 shadow docket decisions during the summer recess covering various political and administrative issues.
