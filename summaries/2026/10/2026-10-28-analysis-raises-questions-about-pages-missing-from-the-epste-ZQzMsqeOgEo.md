# Analysis Raises Questions About Pages Missing From the Epstein Files

- **Published:** 2026-10-28
- **Channel:** CBS News
- **Video:** [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZQzMsqeOgEo)

## Summary
An analysis of the Epstein files released by the Department of Justice reveals numerical gaps and missing pages, raising questions about whether documents were withheld or incorrectly coded as duplicative.

## Major Developments
### 1. Numerical Gaps and Missing Pages in Epstein Files
By analyzing Bates numbers and tracking sequences within the Epstein files released under the Epstein Files Transparency Act, investigators identified thousands of missing pages and document gaps.

**Why it matters:** The discrepancies and missing pages undermine public trust in the government's handling and release of the documents mandated by Congress.

### 2. DOJ Explanation and Coding Errors
The DOJ stated that it released all required documents, attributing missing files and emails to unrelated items, privileges, duplications, or coding errors where documents were mistakenly marked as duplicative.

**Why it matters:** Explains the official stance of the Department of Justice regarding the missing records and provides context to the public scrutiny over withheld information.

## Key Takeaways
- The Department of Justice collected 6 million pages and released 3.5 million pages under the Epstein Files Transparency Act.
- Analysis using Bates numbers identified tens of thousands of missing pages, including missing email chains and financial check records.
- The DOJ attributed some missing documents to coding errors that mistakenly marked files as duplicative.
- Persistent questions and gaps in the released documents continue to challenge public trust in government transparency regarding Epstein-related matters.
